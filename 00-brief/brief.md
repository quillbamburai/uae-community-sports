# Brief — Community Sports Booking

UAE government entity. Product design challenge.

## The situation

A community sports facility (courts and pitches for football, tennis, padel,
badminton) has seen bookings fall from **2,100/month to 780/month** — while
actual attendance stayed high.

## Why

1. **No enforcement.** People walk in and take courts, including booked ones.
2. **No incentive to book.** Booking offers no guarantee the court will be free.
3. **No-shows.** Around a third of bookings never turn up, leaving courts
   sitting empty but reserved.

## The ask

An actual interface and experience — not journey maps or service blueprints.
A working, clickable prototype. Mobile-first, realistic mock data, no backend.

Must be demoable as a 25-minute case study, including where and how AI was used
in the process.

## The solution shape

Make the booking **guaranteed and worth it** — visible enforcement, a trust
score, and perks. Make it **obvious at the point of arrival** whether a court is
legitimately taken.

## Two products, not one

| | |
|---|---|
| **The app** | Phone. The customer's. Browse, book, confirm, trust score. |
| **The kiosk** | Fixed touchscreen at each court. The enforcement mechanism. |

These are separate experiences with different constraints — the kiosk is read
from standing distance and never held in a hand.

## Scope priority

**High — full detail**
1. App home
2. Court/slot selection + booking flow
3. Kiosk (the court-entry touchscreen)
4. Walk-in / no-booking flow

**Medium — functional but simple**
5. Court detail
6. Payment/confirmation

**Low — one UI element each, not full flows**
- Group booking credits / "in the pot" / shared spend
- Complimentary rewards over time
- Subscription (mention only)

## Design direction

- Mobile-first, clean, government-appropriate — trustworthy, not playful
- Trust score and perks visible and rewarding **without looking gamified**.
  This is a civic utility, not a game.
- Follow the sketches for structure and hierarchy; refine rather than reinvent
